# Case Study: Building Compliance Into a Cloud Infrastructure Program

**Role:** Staff / Sr. Technical Program Manager
**Context:** Two companies — a global data/credit-services company and a global technology platform company

## Situation

Two different points in my career put me in the same position: leading technical programs where regulatory and compliance risk was not a side consideration, it was the primary constraint. At one company, that meant SOX, PCI, and SOC compliance reviews tied to infrastructure transformation. At another, it meant building EU Digital Services Act (DSA) compliance into a content platform from the ground up.

## Task

In both cases, I had to make sure technical delivery didn't outrun the compliance and audit requirements around it — without slowing programs down to the point where they stopped shipping.

## Action

**Compliance reviews during infrastructure transformation:**
- Led SOX, PCI, and SOC compliance reviews with external auditors, partnering directly with the security team to validate control effectiveness across the infrastructure transformation program.
- Led a team of project/program managers driving the company's total infrastructure transformation, running executive steering committees specifically on security, audit, and compliance.

**Building DSA compliance into a live platform:**
- Architected moderation thresholds and transparency-reporting frameworks for content review and appeals workstreams to meet EU Digital Services Act requirements.
- Separately led SOX compliance reviews for the same organization, partnering with Finance and Internal Audit to validate control effectiveness.

## Result

- Passed all external SOX, PCI, and SOC audits with no material findings tied to the transformation program.
- Delivered EU DSA compliance architecture ahead of the regulatory deadline, with a transparency-reporting framework the company's legal team could stand behind.

## What I'd tell you if you asked in an interview

Compliance work has a specific failure mode for TPMs: treating it as a checkbox that gets bolted onto the end of a program rather than a set of requirements that shapes the program's architecture from day one. The DSA program is the clearer example — the moderation thresholds weren't a compliance layer added on top of the content system, they were part of how the system was designed to work.
