# Taking an AI Assistant from Insight to Trusted Action

> **Sanitized portfolio case study.** This document synthesizes my AI-enablement and platform-program experience. Company, product, system, and stakeholder identities have been removed.

**Role:** Staff Technical Program Manager

**Relevant domain:** Enterprise AI enablement, knowledge systems, cross-product platform delivery
**Program theme:** An embedded AI assistant succeeds when useful intelligence, safe actions, reliable platform integrations, and user trust mature together.

## Situation

A global technology company was expanding AI-assisted work across engineering and business teams while also consolidating several acquired and internally built products onto a shared platform. Teams could generate useful answers, but knowledge was repeatedly re-created, program context was fragmented, and action still depended on people manually moving information between tools and workflows.

## Task

I led program work that addressed both sides of the problem: making AI-generated knowledge reusable across the company and validating an internal assistant against a complex, multi-workstream program. In parallel, I coordinated the platform consolidation and managed API capabilities needed to support reliable, programmatic action across products.

## Action

- Drove a company-wide knowledge-capture initiative that converted AI-assisted work sessions into reusable context for engineering and non-engineering teams.
- Piloted an internal AI knowledge assistant against a live, complex program, validating its ability to answer questions about status, milestones, decisions, and workstream history.
- Defined a feedback loop for answer quality and missing context so gaps became improvements to source data and governance, not just isolated assistant failures.
- Coordinated seven workstreams and 150+ contributors across five products during a shared-platform consolidation, aligning product, engineering, legal, finance, marketing, and enablement dependencies.
- Helped deliver managed API and command-line capabilities as part of the platform program, creating a path from information retrieval toward controlled, programmatic action.
- Used phased rollout principles: validate with bounded use cases, observe quality and operational risk, then expand audiences and capabilities.

## Result

- Expanded reusable AI knowledge practices beyond engineering toward company-wide adoption.
- Demonstrated that a knowledge assistant could reduce repetitive program-status requests when grounded in governed, current source material.
- Reduced the need to re-derive the same context in each AI session, improving efficiency and lowering avoidable AI usage cost.
- Created a repeatable program model for scaling AI capabilities alongside the platform, data, permissions, and operating controls they depend on.

## Transferable operating principles

The TPM challenge is broader than shipping a conversational interface: each new capability needs dependable data, explicit permissions, safe execution, observability, rollback paths, and a rollout plan that earns user trust. I organize delivery around end-to-end outcomes—for example, detect an operational issue, explain the recommendation, obtain the right confirmation, execute a bounded change, and verify the result across affected systems.

## What I'd tell you if you asked in an interview

AI assistants become valuable when they stop being impressive demos and become dependable parts of a workflow. The program discipline is to treat answer quality, action safety, data freshness, permissions, and user adoption as one integrated delivery problem rather than five separate launches.
