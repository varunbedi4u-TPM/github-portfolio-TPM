# Case Study: Unifying Five Products Into One Platform

**Role:** Staff Technical Program Manager
**Context:** A global technology platform company (mobile/ads industry)

## Situation

The company had grown through a mix of acquisition and internal product development, resulting in five separate advertising products — each with its own dashboard, data model, and customer-facing experience. Customers using more than one product had to log into multiple systems with no unified view. Leadership set a company-wide priority to unify all five into a single modernized dashboard.

## Task

I was asked to lead the program end-to-end: seven workstreams, 150+ contributors, five product and engineering leads, each with different priorities, release cadences, and technical debt. My job was to own rollout sequencing, GA strategy per product, and the governance that kept the launch from becoming five uncoordinated efforts wearing one name.

## Action

- Built a governance process that got design, go-to-market strategy, documentation, and legal review signed off across all five products in lockstep with delivery — instead of each product team running its own approval process on its own timeline.
- Designed a migration flywheel that started with a 15-organization pilot wave, validated the approach, then scaled to production waves of 10,000+ organizations at a time.
- Coordinated beyond engineering — legal, finance, marketing, account management, and product enablement all had dependencies on the sequencing decisions I owned.
- When early GA timelines slipped, I used the slip as a forcing function to tighten workstream dependency mapping rather than just pushing the date and hoping the same gaps didn't recur.

## Result

- Migrated 3.11 million non-revenue-generating organizations and 14,483 revenue-generating organizations — representing $26.8M in active spend — within two months of GA, with no revenue disruption.
- Delivered a single, modernized dashboard experience across all five products, replacing five fragmented ones.
- The governance model became the reference pattern other cross-product programs at the company now use for multi-team launch sequencing.

## What I'd tell you if you asked in an interview

The hardest part wasn't the technical migration — it was that none of the five product teams reported to the same leader, so I had no formal authority to force alignment. What worked was making the governance process itself valuable enough that teams wanted to be in it, rather than trying to mandate compliance.
