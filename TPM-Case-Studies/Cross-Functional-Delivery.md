# Case Study: Migrating Enterprise Customers Off Legacy Infrastructure

**Role:** Sr. Program Manager, Infrastructure & Cloud
**Context:** A global data and credit-services company

## Situation

Hundreds of enterprise customers — including major banks and telecom providers — were connected to the company's systems via legacy mainframe-to-mainframe integration. This created data-center dependencies that blocked a broader cloud transformation initiative and left the company exposed to aging infrastructure with no clear decommission plan.

## Task

I owned the migration of these enterprise customers off mainframe connectivity onto a modern API architecture, and separately led a full decommissioning effort for legacy hardware across two data centers — coordinating vendors, security teams, and the customers themselves without causing service disruption to any of them.

## Action

- Partnered directly with client-side engineering teams at each bank and telecom provider to plan and execute their individual migration off mainframe-to-mainframe connectivity onto API-based architecture.
- Led further networking initiatives that enabled the broader cloud transformation program by removing data-center dependencies tied to legacy connections.
- Orchestrated a one-week decommissioning blitz across primary and secondary data centers, coordinating a 30-person team spanning five vendors to securely retire legacy systems and dispose of 4,000+ hardware components and PII-bearing drives on-site.
- Sequenced vendor and data-migration timelines carefully to avoid disruption to other customers co-located in the same active, multi-customer data center.

## Result

- Migrated 100s of enterprise customers off legacy mainframe connectivity with zero service disruption.
- Retired legacy infrastructure for $23M in combined annual savings.
- Completed the entire decommissioning blitz with zero incidents in a live, multi-tenant data center — a constraint that made "close enough" execution not an option.

## What I'd tell you if you asked in an interview

The riskiest part wasn't the technical migration — it was that we were operating in a data center other customers were actively using. Any misstep in sequencing could have taken down infrastructure that wasn't even ours to touch. That's what drove the level of vendor and timeline coordination on this one.
