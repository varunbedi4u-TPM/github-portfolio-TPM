60-SECOND CASE STUDY · 2025–2026

When the original cloud strategy stopped making economic sense.

THE FRICTION

Three platform groups across two product organizations had different infrastructure leaders, support models, migration costs, and incentives. The original mandate favored convergence on an existing GKE-based platform, but alignment on the destination did not eliminate the economics or autonomy concerns.

THE HARD CALL

When vendor pricing changed the business case, I helped redirect the program from consolidation to making the established platform portable across GCP and AWS. That meant discarding part of the original plan and accepting greater near-term engineering complexity.

HOW I BROKE THE STALEMATE

I reset the charter around shared reliability, latency, support, and revenue guardrails; rebuilt the dependency model; and created one decision forum where each organization could expose constraints without losing local technical ownership.

DECISION OUTCOMEA multi-cloud direction that preserved strategic flexibility while keeping the 99.9% uptime KPI and business guardrails explicit.

HOW I OPERATE

The programs change.
The principles don’t.

The recurring work is making complexity navigable: creating enough structure for decisions and delivery without creating bureaucracy that slows both.

  1. 01

    Turn ambiguity into an operating system.

    Surface the decision, trade-off, and consequence—not a longer dashboard.

  3. 03

    Scale through mechanisms, not heroics.
